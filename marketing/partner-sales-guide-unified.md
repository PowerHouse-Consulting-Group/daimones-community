## 1. What daïmōnes is (in one minute)

daïmōnes is a sovereign AI reasoning platform. It runs on infrastructure the client controls, reasons transparently from source corpora the client defines, and cites every claim so that outputs can be verified. It does not sanitize conclusions to match a corporate acceptable-use policy. The first public persona — Aristotle, grounded in the Corpus Aristotelicum in polytonic Ancient Greek — demonstrates that AI can do rigorous research without corporate content filtering.

**The one-liner for every conversation:**

> "Uncensored in reasoning, transparent in method."

**The short pitch:**

> "Most AI answers from an inoffensive average. daïmōnes answers from your corpus, with a full audit trail."

## 5. Shared first-tier Q&A (both products)

**What makes daïmōnes different from ChatGPT, Claude, or enterprise assistants?**
Closed models are black boxes optimized for broad acceptability. daïmōnes is optimized for intellectual honesty: a transparent reasoning layer that retrieves from your corpus, shows its chain of thought, and does not outsource editorial judgment to a third-party vendor.

**Is it safe for politically sensitive work?**
Safety here means traceability, not silence. daïmōnes does not hide controversial conclusions; it documents the reasoning and sources behind them. Editorial responsibility sits with the client organization, exactly as with any other research product.

**How does it handle citations and provenance?**
Every answer is tied to specific passages in the corpus. Outputs include source references, confidence indicators, and the reasoning chain used — supporting reproducibility and peer review.

**Is it compliant with the EU AI Act?**
Yes. daïmōnes is built on the AI Act's two pillars: transparency and human oversight. Audit logs, AI-content labeling, and human-in-the-loop review are native features.

**How does it handle AI watermarking requirements?**
The platform embeds provenance metadata and machine-readable markers in AI-generated outputs, so the client can demonstrate to reviewers, funders, or regulators which portions of a report were AI-assisted and which were human-edited. Watermarking requirements are not a contradiction of sovereign positioning — they are a feature we ship.

**Who owns the data and outputs?**
The client owns everything. We never train on client data, and in a Lyceum in a Box deployment we cannot access the outputs at all.

**How fast can we deploy?**
Platform onboarding is near-immediate for pilot programs. A Lyceum in a Box pilot typically takes 2–4 weeks after corpus review and infrastructure assessment; a limited evaluation sandbox can often be made available sooner.

**What does it cost?**
Platform: tiered academic programs, quoted by the team. Lyceum in a Box: project-based, quoted by PowerHouse Consulting Group. Partners never quote figures themselves.

## 6. What not to say (both products)

- Do not claim daïmōnes replaces subject-matter experts. It augments research; reviewers and editors remain in charge.
- Do not promise zero errors. Promise transparency: every claim is traceable and correctable.
- Do not claim daïmōnes is "unregulated." It is compliant-by-design and supports human oversight.
- Do not present it as a way to bypass academic-integrity rules or as a disinformation tool. It is a transparency instrument for legitimate research.
- Never quote prices from memory, and never invent price points. Route to the correct contact.
- Never mix the two commercial lines in one conversation: universities talk to architect@daimones.ai, on-premise/custom deployments talk to PowerHouse.
- Avoid inflammatory or conspiratorial framing. Keep the conversation institutional, technical, and evidence-based.
