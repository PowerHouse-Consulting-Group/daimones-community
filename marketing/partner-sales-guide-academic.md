## 3. First-tier questions and answers

### What makes daïmōnes different from ChatGPT, Claude, or institutional chatbot wrappers?

Closed models are black boxes trained to avoid controversy. daïmōnes is a transparent reasoning layer you host yourself. It retrieves from your corpus, shows its chain of thought, and does not outsource editorial judgment to a third-party vendor.

### Is it suitable for humanities research?

Yes. The platform is designed for source languages, long-form reasoning, and citation. The Aristotle proof-of-concept handles polytonic Ancient Greek and can be adapted to Latin, Byzantine Greek, Arabic, and other research languages.

### Does it work offline or on university servers?

Yes. Sovereign deployment runs entirely inside your environment — no external API calls, no data leakage to commercial providers. This satisfies most university IT and data-protection requirements.

### How does it handle citations and provenance?

Every answer is tied to specific passages in your corpus. Outputs include source references, confidence indicators, and the reasoning chain used. This supports academic standards for reproducibility and peer review.

### What does it cost?

Academic and research pricing is tailored to deployment size and corpus complexity. Pilot deployments for single departments or labs start below the enterprise rate; multi-year or consortium licenses are available. Contact architect@daimones.ai for a detailed quote.

### How does daïmōnes help with EU grant applications?

It directly supports common Horizon Europe and Digital Europe requirements: open science, FAIR data principles, human oversight, algorithmic transparency, and data sovereignty. A daïmōnes deployment can be described as auditable, self-hosted AI infrastructure with reproducible outputs.

### Is it compliant with the EU AI Act?

Yes. daïmōnes is built for transparency and human oversight, the two pillars of the AI Act. For research outputs that include AI-generated text, images, or synthetic data, the platform can apply metadata labeling and provenance records to help institutions meet Article 50 transparency obligations.

### How does daïmōnes handle AI watermarking requirements?

The platform can embed provenance metadata and machine-readable markers in AI-generated research outputs. This means your institution can demonstrate — to reviewers, funders, or regulators — which portions of a report, dataset, or publication were AI-assisted and which were human-edited.

## 5. What not to say

- Do not claim daïmōnes replaces subject-matter experts. It augments research; reviewers and editors remain in charge.
- Do not promise zero errors. Promise transparency: every claim is traceable and correctable.
- Do not present it as a way to bypass academic integrity rules. It is a tool for documented, accountable scholarship.
- Avoid framing that sounds anti-technology. Position it as better, more rigorous AI for serious research.
